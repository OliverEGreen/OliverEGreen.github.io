Ready

Ready is a design system after Dieter Rams. It began with the lettering on an old computer’s keys. It has its own typeface, and a keyboard you can play.

1 · Where it starts

A home computer’s keys

Ready grew out of the lettering on a keyboard, and a way of designing that asks for less.

The Commodore 64 was a home computer from 1982. The lettering on its keycaps was plain and square, and easy to read.

C64 Keyboard is a free font by Levente Szabadkai, drawn from that lettering. It is released under CC0, so anyone can build on it.

  1. 1Ready Condensed keeps the keycap capitals and adds a new lowercase.
  2. 2Ready is Ready Condensed widened to 124 per cent. The stems stay the same and the spacing opens to 108 per cent.
  3. 3The rest of the system follows Dieter Rams, who designed for Braun from 1955.
Less, but better.Dieter Rams

On the ET 66 calculator, one key is yellow: the equals. Ready keeps the same habit with one colour, orange.

3 · Our keyboard

A keyboard you can play

Ready’s keys, set out as a keyboard: dark, sculpted keys in a beige case. They lift as you pass over them and push in when you click or tap. Click the keyboard and your own keys press them too.

4 · The design system

The design system

Ready is a set of parts: keys, fields, switches, sliders, menus, tables and cards. These pieces put them to work.

Photo tableSort, filter and group a raised table of photos. Photo libraryA tree of photos under folder tabs, with details beside it. Design galleryBraun and Vitsoe designs in Ready’s cards. KeyboardThe keyboard above, on a page of its own. Control panelToggles, sliders and steppers that read back as plain words. Collector censusA five-step form with hints instead of red errors. Command palettePress ⌘K and search the catalogue.

Out and in

Buttons come out of the page and inputs go in. You can tell what to press and where to type before you read a word.

One colour

Orange means on, chosen or live. Nothing else in the interface is coloured, so it is easy to find.

Small labels

Labels are small and in sentence case. They sit above what they name and stay quiet.
