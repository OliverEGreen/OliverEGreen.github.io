I have been vibe coding since January this year, with Claude being my daily driver for work since March. Barely halfway through, and what a year it's been. I'd like to reflect on that.

It's clear that today's LLMs are *incredible tools*. They are allowing us to achieve way, *way* more than was ever possible before now. The recent past has become almost unrecognisable; yesteryear's paradigms and expectations for software development look almost comically dated.

We've hit July now and the dust has yet to really settle. It feels like every week – every day – we're witnessing the genius work machine transform before our eyes: research tools, agentic loops, recursive self-improvement, flexible (and tasteful!) design tools. And all of this change compounds. As if we're on a rollercoaster, we've lost the sense of controlling our own destiny; the best we can do is hold on tight and try to keep our wits about us. 

As such, this post is really just the interval that lands at the dramatic highpoint of a play. A moment to reflect, ponder on what comes next and catch our collective breaths. And to hunt down those snacks in the lobby.

## Out with the old

The old software development lifecycle is hanging on by a thread; more of a traditional ceremony now than a vital part of everyone's weekly routines.

The old roles are dead, too. I've seen managers, PMs and even software developers spin up rich, interactive prototypes in just a few hours of tinkering. What used to take a trained specialist days has now collapsed into the abyss. All we do now is tinker and produce, someone called us "AI slop cannons" and there's a harsh truth to that.

The tech sector has enjoyed maybe two or three decades of relative stability:

* We knew who did what role, and we knew how to stick to that.
* We had a reasonable sense of what our career trajectory might look like.
* We knew what things were worth.

Again, this looks comically dated now. And nobody will really mourn us; next to lawyers, we were poster children of the slow, ivory-tower genius class that arguably earned far too much money when compared with teachers or nurses.

It was too good to last.

For everyone else, our loss is their gain – software will surely become easier to use, effortlessly customisable, instantly generated on-demand and essentially free. Code may flow as easily as water or electricity as just another public utility.

## Where the rot is

As of July 2026, software developers are still stuck in limbo. The new world I've been describing hasn't fully manifested just yet. And so we're coding away faster and more prolifically than ever before.

Nobody I know writes lines of code by hand anymore. Some of them have stopped even checking their generated outputs if everything seems to be working. The machine is getting better. Anthropic suggested Claude Code would surpass human-level engineer output before the end of 2026. Given the speed and trajectory of this product class, there's little reason to cast any doubt this claim.

And there is something deeply *acerbic* about this new way of working. I've been trying to put my finger on why: 

* The lack of dignity. Vonnegut captured this beautifully in Player Piano [quote]
* A sense of something rotting away the heart and soul. Overnight, tech has become meaningless. The day job is now bleak.
* Pride has gone. As has craft.

In speaking with others, there seem to be a surprising consensus; the short intervals of watching your LLM of choice perform its ritualistic 'thinking' dance have become surprisingly unbearable. It's rarely taking more than 10 minutes to accomplish a full day of work, and yet it's somehow pain. Have we become unreasonable? 

I've responded by undertaking small chores - unloading the dishwasher, watering the plants, going on a short walk if the LLM is really thinking hard. I keep workout equipment by my desk and perform all manner of exercises while I wait for the machine to do its thing.

## Could the fix be worse?

And what about when these models invitably speed up and become instant? Could this be worse?

Apple chess introduced a small wait, mostly for UX reasons. Something to save the sanity and ego of the user. We already parse through mountains of LLM-generated text every day now. The feedback loop will eventually speed up: 

What about when it's instant?

- Humans talking out loud into horrifying cyberpunk mouth tubes. Suitably dystopian-looking, honestly top marks to the designer who so-perfectly captured our current zeitgeist.
- LLMs instantly respond with 10 paragraphs of perfectly formatted response. 

The brain fries almost instantly. We've probably evolved to handle large amounts of downtimeand boredom. We could be consuming the ancient equivalent of lifetimes of knowledge in an afternoon's work.

The bottleneck will soon become the speed and capacity of the human mind. The LLMs will jump in - as they already are - to lift the burden from us even more, predicting our next move, our preferences, based on its prior data and mapping of our minds.

We become an appendage to the machine, withering away and looking ever more useless. Like a biological appendix, we slowly become a potential hazard. 

## Conclusion?

I don't have any clever points to make, nor original observations to offer. Work has always for many people been a way to pay the bills. I'm one of a few - perhaps its our character class of humans - that has always taken a fastidious and unusual level of pride in their work. I love learning and I love teaching others. I love hard challenges and I love design in its many new forms. 

Inexplicably, it feels like this has somehow been taken away from me. 

Software development is easier than ever now, but it *weighs nothing*.

These are the core themes that land at the heart of the Vonnegut novel - What comes of our dignity? Our identity? How does society respond when these are stripped away from us?

Viktor Frankl. Man's Quest for Meaning.  

There are no easy answers. "The time is ripe for cults". Or maybe the only truly modern, genuine way forward is for us to transcend the limits of our monkey brains entirely and embrace transhumanism.
